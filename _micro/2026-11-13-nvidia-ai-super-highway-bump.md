_**A big NVIDIA bump on AI super highway**_

The CEO of the most critical AI chip company in the world is out, and the whole thing unfolded in a 96-hour span. Whatever the scandal specifics, the deeper story is the one-man risk that Nvidia and its board apparently never saw, or never acted on. Mr. Huang had dozens of employees (60?) reporting straight to him, and the company no formal succession plan, and no bench of public-facing executives ready to step into the top role. That's the glaring part: not just that the risk existed, but that it was so visible and still went unmitigated.

And it isn't just Nvidia's problem to handle now after the Founder/CEO resignation. [Mohamed El-Erian posted on X](https://x.com/elerianm/status/2120655409488789804?a=L_AKnrlg-USyvTSySaM&s=20) the night before Huang resigned:

> "This latest development challenges that assumption in an environment in which<br>
> This is no ordinary CEO<br>
> This is no ordinary company<br>
> This is no ordinary sector<br>
> This is no ordinary time.<br>
> With so much levered to all this, and with so much uncertainty in play, it's hard to see the effects contained to just the company."

Hard to disagree. A bump this size on the AI super highway doesn't stay in one lane. Many of the experts analysis is that it ripples through every company whose roadmap, valuation, or supply chain assumed Nvidia's CEO is going to be there forever. At least in the short 2-3 year term.
