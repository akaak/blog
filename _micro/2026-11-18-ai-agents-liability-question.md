_**AI Agents and the Liability Question**_

AI agents are running into a new hurdle: legal review. Who pays when the agent gets it wrong? It's an emerging pattern, and even the builders are saying it out loud. Anthropic's IPO filing warns that a catastrophic AI incident could be too big for the insurance industry to cover. The EU and California already treat AI like a product. Without a history of AI Agents in the business, insurers write exclusions, and the risk goes back to whoever builds or deploys the agent.

**For companies buying agents:** ask what happens when the agent gets it wrong. Who is liable, how much is covered, what triggers the coverage, and is it written into the contract? One has to balance the autonomy with humans in control to reap the benefits.

**For vendors selling to the enterprise:** a better demo won't get you past legal. A defined limit, a defined trigger and a policy that actually pays out will. To get insured, you have to show you're in control: tight permissions, an audit trail, real testing and a _kill switch_.

Insurance doesn't remove liability or the need for oversight. It shows who covers what, so humans review the decisions that really matter.
